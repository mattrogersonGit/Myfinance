Why bother?

Why use a budget app?

When household spending is spread across an everyday account, a credit card, and the odd cash purchase, it's genuinely hard to know where the money actually goes. Bank statements show individual transactions, but not the bigger picture — how much you're really spending on groceries, on power, or on the mortgage, month to month.

A budgeting tool pulls that spending into one place and groups it into categories you actually recognise, so you can see patterns, spot where a budget is slipping, and make decisions with real numbers instead of a rough guess.

A common alternative

How it compares to a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet can absolutely work for household budgeting — plenty of people track their finances this way. But it usually means building your own categories, writing your own formulas, and manually re-entering transactions each month to keep it up to date.

MyFinance is built specifically around personal and household finance tracking: importing a CSV, categorising transactions, and seeing budgets, debt, investments and net worth without setting any of that up yourself. It won't suit everyone, but if maintaining a spreadsheet has become a chore, it's a more purpose-built alternative.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is a budget app?

A budget app is a tool that helps you record and organise your income and spending so you can see where your money goes each month, set spending limits by category, and track progress toward savings or debt goals.

Is MyFinance available in New Zealand?

Yes. MyFinance is built for New Zealand households — figures are shown in NZD, and CSV imports from ANZ, ASB, BNZ and Westpac are recognised automatically, with a generic CSV importer for other banks.

Do I need to connect my bank account?

No. MyFinance never asks for your online banking login and never connects directly to your bank. You export a transaction CSV from your own internet banking and import it into MyFinance yourself.

Can I import bank transactions?

Yes. Export a CSV from your bank's internet banking and import it into MyFinance. ANZ, ASB, BNZ and Westpac formats are recognised automatically, and a generic importer covers other banks that export a date, description and amount.

What can I track with MyFinance?

MyFinance covers everyday spending, category budgets, recurring payments, savings, debt and mortgages, investments, and overall net worth — all in one place.

Is MyFinance suitable for household budgeting?

Yes. You can track personal and household spending in one account, and Pro accounts can invite another household member, such as a partner, to share the same data.

How do I get started, and what does it cost?

You can start using MyFinance straight away without creating an account. Core budgeting features are free — see our pricing page for full details on the free and Pro plans.

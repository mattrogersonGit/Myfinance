Money Conversations

"Kids are surrounded by money every day. The best lessons often happen in ordinary moments."

You don't need to sit children down for a formal financial lesson. Money can be discussed while it's already part of what you're doing.

Finding the balance

Don't hide every money decision

Children don't need to know every detail of the family's finances. But completely hiding money can make it seem mysterious. Explain age-appropriate decisions openly, and avoid discussing adult financial stress in ways that make children feel responsible for it.

It's okay to admit it

Talk about mistakes

"I made a bad money decision."

"I wish I'd thought about that first."

This shows children that adults make mistakes too.

Language matters

Don't make money shameful

Avoid

"We can't afford that because we're bad with money."

Instead

"That's not something we're choosing to spend our money on."

Help children understand that households have different priorities and circumstances.

More than numbers

Money and values

Money conversations are also conversations about values. There's no single correct answer.

The one to remember

A simple rule

Answer the question they're asking — then answer the question behind it.

Child asks: "Why can't I have that?"
Real question: "How do we decide what we spend money on?"
A simple routine

Weekly money chat

Once a week, five minutes is enough:

  1. Count savings
  2. Check goals
  3. Discuss upcoming spending
  4. Distribute pocket money
  5. Talk about one money idea

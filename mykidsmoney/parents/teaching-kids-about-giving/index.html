Teaching Kids About Giving

"Money isn't only for buying things for ourselves. Giving can be one of the most rewarding things children learn to do with it."

Generosity is easier to understand when children are actually given the opportunity to make decisions about giving — not just told to do it.

It doesn't need to be much

Start small

Children don't need to give large amounts — even a small amount can teach the concept.

💛
Give
$1

In our 3-jar system, $1 of the weekly $5 goes into Give. Families can choose whatever amount or percentage works for them.

Ownership matters

Let children choose

Where possible, let children have some control over where their giving goes. The important part is that the child understands what their money is helping with.

School fundraiser A charity Helping another child Community causes An animal organisation A local appeal
For younger children

Make giving tangible

Physical money helps. Let them count what they have, decide how much to give, put it aside, and physically hand it over — a real action, not an abstract concept.

A real trade-off

Giving vs spending

Have $5, give $1$4 left for other purposes

Giving is a different choice from spending — an opportunity to teach that money is limited and choices have consequences.

Beyond money

Giving doesn't need to be money

Generosity can also involve time, effort, belongings, helping someone, or volunteering. Money is simply one way to practise giving.

Parent callout

Don't shame children if they don't want to give. The aim is developing generosity, empathy and thoughtful decision-making — children can learn by seeing adults give as well.

Make it real

Show them the impact

Where possible, help children understand what their contribution does.

"$5 helped support this school fundraiser."
"Your donation helped provide something for someone who needed it."

Keep examples realistic — don't promise a specific outcome unless it's actually known.

Try it together

Family giving

Give each child a small amount of money and let them choose a cause. Discuss: why did you choose it? Who does it help? What do you think your money will do? The goal is conversation, not the size of the donation.

Building the routine

Giving as a habit

Regular small acts of generosity can be easier for children to understand than occasional large donations. That's why a Give jar can be useful — the money gradually builds up until there's an opportunity to give it.

Where it starts

Start with real money

For younger children, start with physical money — coins, jars, pocket money, savings goals, real purchases. Children should experience the concepts rather than simply read about them.

Use the lessons

The lessons are designed to build knowledge progressively — but don't sit a child down and make them complete dozens in a row. Instead: choose a topic, read it together, discuss it, connect it to something happening in real life, and let the child try it.

Set the tone

Don't turn it into homework

MyKidsMoney should feel like exploration, not schoolwork. Keep sessions short — one idea at a time. Ask questions. Let children disagree. Use real examples. The goal is understanding, not completing the most lessons.

Better than a lecture

Ask questions

"What would you do?" "Why?" "What would happen if...?" "Would you still buy it?" "How long would you need to save?" "Is it worth it?" "What could go wrong?"

This encourages financial thinking rather than memorisation.

Part of the process

Make mistakes safe

Children should have opportunities to make small, manageable money mistakes. Spending $5 on something they don't really want can teach more than a lecture about impulse buying. The aim isn't to prevent every mistake — it's to help children learn from them.

Patience & Money

Sometimes waiting today gives you more choices tomorrow.

The basics

Saving takes time

A goal costs $30. You save $5 each week. It takes 6 weeks to reach it. Waiting is just part of saving for something bigger — it isn't a problem to solve.

Why physical money helps

Watching the pile grow

A physical savings jar makes patience easier to practise — you can literally see the coins growing over time, and count your own progress. That's just the practical approach MyKidsMoney uses with younger children, not a claim about how everyone learns best.

In the moment

Handling "I want it now"

Sometimes we want something right away. That's normal — but waiting can give you time to decide whether you actually want it. If you see a $5 toy today, you don't have to buy it immediately. Try asking:

  • Will I still want it tomorrow?
  • Would I rather save the $5?
  • Is there something else I'm already saving for?

This isn't about saying every quick purchase is a bad idea — just that a moment of thought can be useful.

Reaching a goal can feel especially rewarding when you know you worked towards it yourself.

That's okay too

Changing your mind

Kids sometimes decide they no longer want their goal — and that's completely fine. You can choose a different goal, keep saving, or decide to spend some of the money instead. This is about learning through experience, not being punished for changing your mind.

Try it yourself

The one-day wait

Choose something you want that costs less than $10. Before buying it, wait one day. Then decide whether you still want it.

For parents

Rather than just telling your child to be patient, help them actually experience waiting — a savings goal is a natural, low-pressure way to practise it.

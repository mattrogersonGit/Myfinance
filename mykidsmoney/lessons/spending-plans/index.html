A familiar starting point

You already know MyKidsMoney's Spend / Save / Give approach — for example, $5 split into $2 Spend, $2 Save, $1 Give. Another child, or another family, might choose completely different amounts. The important part isn't the exact split — it's having a plan at all.

A real-life scenario

Planning for $15

You have $15. You want a $5 treat, to save towards a $30 goal, to give $1, and to keep some money available. How would you plan it?

There's no single correct answer — try making your own plan and see what feels right.

Why a plan helps

Because money is limited

A plan helps because you can't do everything with the same money at once. This connects directly to Making Choices With Limited Money and Trade-offs.

Not permanent

Plans can change

If something costs more than expected, or you simply change your mind, you can adjust your plan. A budget is a tool, not a rule that can never change.

Try it yourself

My Money Plan

You have $20. How much will you Spend, Save, Give, and keep available? What are you saving for, and why did you choose those amounts?

There's no single correct answer here — the goal is thinking it through and being able to explain your own choices.

For parents

Let your child make their own decisions here. Ask questions rather than telling them what to do — the goal is building confidence and understanding, not landing on the "right" plan.

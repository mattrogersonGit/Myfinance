What Is a Job?

Work you do for someone, in return for something — often money.

The basics

What is a job?

A job is work you do for someone in return for something, often money. People do many different jobs, because a community needs many different skills and services to function.

Doing work, receiving money

Work and payment

🌱You mow someone's lawn.
$10They pay you $10. You provided a service and received money.

That $10 is money coming in — which means it's income.

An important distinction

Not all useful work is paid

Being paid doesn't decide whether work is worthwhile. People also volunteer, help family, contribute to their community, and take on household responsibilities — none of it paid, all of it useful.

Volunteering Helping family Contributing to community Household responsibilities
Two different things

Pocket money and jobs

Regular pocket money and earning extra money for a job aren't quite the same thing. One real family treats a few regular household jobs as just part of contributing, and pays separately for bigger, optional jobs — that's one way to do it, not the only correct system. See the full approach →

Try it yourself

Name three jobs

For each one, think about: what does that person actually do? Who might pay them? And why would someone be willing to pay for that work?

For older children

Jobs can be full-time, part-time, temporary, self-employed or casual. As people get older, ideas like skills, qualifications and experience start to matter more for the kind of work they can do — this lesson isn't about salaries, just about understanding that work takes many different forms.

For parents

Talk with your child about the jobs people do in your community. Try asking:

What problem does this job solve? What service does this person provide?

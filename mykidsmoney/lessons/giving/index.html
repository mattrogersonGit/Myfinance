Giving

Money isn't only for buying things for ourselves.

The basics

What is giving?

We can choose to use some of our money to help others. That's giving — and it's one of the three things money can do, alongside spending and saving.

Making it real

What can giving look like?

Giving can take lots of forms:

Charity School fundraising Helping someone in need Donating to a cause Community organisations

Real examples kids might recognise: Daffodil Day at school, or donating to an organisation that provides pyjamas to children in need. There's no single "correct" cause — what matters is choosing something that means something to your family.

A choice, not an obligation

Giving is voluntary

Nobody has to give. The point of this lesson isn't to say giving is required — it's to help children understand that money can have a purpose beyond themselves, and that generosity is something you can choose.

Try it yourself

How would you use $5?

You have $5. How might you choose to split it between spending, saving and giving? There's no single correct answer — it's your call.

For parents

Talk with your child about causes your family cares about. Where possible, help them understand where their donated money actually goes — connecting giving to a real-world impact makes it far more meaningful than an abstract idea.

A $1 coin dropping into a Give jar is a simple, visible reminder that they've chosen to give.

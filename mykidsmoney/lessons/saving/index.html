Saving

Choosing not to spend some money now, so you can use it later.

The basics

What is saving?

Saving means choosing not to spend some of your money now, so you can use it later.

$5You receive $5.
$2You put $2 into your Save jar.
$3You now have $2 saved and $3 available for other choices.

Seeing the saved money physically sitting there — and growing over time — makes it easy to know exactly how much you've saved.

Give it a reason

Saving works best with a goal

Saving is much easier when there's a reason for it — Bluetooth headphones, a toy, a bike, a special outing, whatever it might be.

Be patient

Saving takes time

If your goal costs $30, and you save $5 a week, it'll take 6 weeks to get there. Bigger goals just take longer — that's normal, not a problem.

Weighing it up

Saving vs. spending

Spend now

You get something today.

Save

You wait, but you might be able to get something bigger later.

Neither is automatically right or wrong — the skill is making the choice on purpose.

Money left in Save can sometimes earn a little more — see how our family uses interest to introduce the idea of compound growth.

For parents

Give your child $10 in physical coins, and ask:

  1. How much would you spend?
  2. How much would you save?
  3. What are you saving for?

From there, help them set up a simple savings goal with a physical jar where practical, and let them count their own progress. Let your child own the goal — it means more when it's genuinely theirs.

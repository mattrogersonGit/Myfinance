🏦 BANKS & DIGITAL MONEY · LESSON 4

What Is Digital Money?

Most of the money people use today is represented digitally in bank accounts.

Starting familiar

From coins to a digital record

🪙🪙🪙🪙🪙Five $1 coins. You can see and touch your $5.
$5Put it in a bank account, and you'll usually see a digital record showing $5, rather than those exact coins.
Everyday examples

Where digital money shows up

When people use a debit card, transfer money, receive wages, pay bills, or shop online, those transactions are generally recorded electronically.

Digital money isn't imaginary — it represents real money recorded within the financial system, just like a receipt represents a real purchase.

A common mix-up

Digital doesn't mean unlimited

If your account contains $20, having a digital card doesn't mean you have unlimited money. Spend $5, and $15 remains — exactly the same as if you'd spent physical cash.

🪙 Physical

  • Coins and notes
  • Easy to see and count
  • You physically hand them over

💻 Digital

  • Numbers in an account
  • Payments happen electronically
  • Easy to lose track of spending

Neither is universally better — they're just different ways of representing the same real money.

Growing up with money

Physical first, digital later

Younger children can benefit from learning with physical money first, since it's easier to see and understand. As children get older, digital money becomes increasingly important because much of everyday spending happens electronically.

For parents

Explain digital money during normal transactions — for example, "We're paying for the groceries with the card. The money is coming out of our bank account." Just avoid exposing children to private banking information.

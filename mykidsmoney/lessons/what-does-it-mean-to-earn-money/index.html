💪 EARNING MONEY · LESSON 2

What Does It Mean to Earn Money?

Earning money means receiving money because you provided work, a service, a product, or something of value.

Earned or just received?

Two different things

Sometimes money comes to you because you did something — that's earning. Sometimes it comes to you because someone gave it to you — that's receiving. Both are real money, but they're not the same.

💪 Earned

  • Washing a car
  • Mowing a lawn
  • Babysitting (when age appropriate)
  • Doing extra work
  • Selling something
  • Working a job

🎁 Received

  • Birthday money
  • A gift
  • Pocket money
  • Money passed on by someone else

Pocket money is a little of both, depending on the family — some families connect it to jobs, some don't. Every family does it differently, and there's no single right way.

Why people get paid

Effort and value

People are generally paid because their work provides something another person values — not simply because the work was hard. Harder work doesn't always mean more money, and someone who earns more isn't more valuable as a person. Not every useful task needs to involve payment at all.

A real example

Washing a car

🚗You wash a car. The person gets a clean car.
$10You receive $10. You provided a service and were paid for it.

Money earned is one possible source of income.

Try it yourself

Earned, received — or could it be either?

A birthday gift

Received — someone gave it to you, not in exchange for work.

Washing a car

Earned — you did the work and were paid for it.

Selling an old toy

Earned — you provided something someone else wanted and they paid you for it.

Weekly pocket money

Could be either — it depends on the family. Some connect it to jobs, some don't.

Working in a shop

Earned — that's a job, and you're paid for the work you do.

Winning a prize

Received — it wasn't payment for work, it was a result of luck or a competition.

For parents

Use everyday examples as they come up. Try asking:

What did the person provide? Why were they paid?
For older children

As people get older, ideas like skills, productivity, experience and qualifications start to shape what someone can earn — because they shape how much value that person can provide. Keep it about providing value, not about comparing people.

Picking a goal

What makes a good goal?

A useful savings goal should be:

  • Meaningful to you
  • Specific — a real thing, with a real price
  • Something you can actually work towards
  • Realistic for your age
It's okay

Waiting — and changing your mind

Knowing exactly what you're working towards can make the waiting easier. But sometimes you change your mind about what you want — and that's completely fine. You can pick a different goal, keep saving, or decide to spend some of it instead.

If you spend your savings on one goal, that same money won't be available for a different goal. That's a trade-off worth thinking about.

Leaving your savings alone can also help you reach a goal faster — see how our family uses interest to introduce the idea of compound growth.

For parents

Help with realistic prices and the maths, but let your child own the goal — it should be genuinely theirs, not something you've chosen for them. Encouragement helps; constant pressure to save doesn't. The real learning happens through experience, not being told what to do.

Counting Money

Once you know coins and notes, the next step is working out how much you actually have.

Starting simple

Start with the same coin

The easiest place to start is counting the same coin over and over:

1 × $1 = $1
2 × $1 = $2
5 × $1 = $5
Mixing it up

Then try mixed coins

Once that feels easy, try adding different coins together:

$2 + $1 + $1 = $4
$2 + $2 + $1 = $5
$5 + $2 + $1 = $8
A few tricks

Counting strategies

Four simple steps

  • Sort the coins by value first
  • Count the $1 coins one at a time
  • Start with the biggest coin when it's mixed
  • Check the total again to be sure

Worked example

Counting $5 + $2 + $2 + $1, starting with the biggest coin:

$5 → $7 → $9 → $10
Why physical money helps

Lay it out and see it

Physically laying coins out on a table while counting lets a child see exactly what they're adding up — every coin is right there, instead of being an abstract number. It's simply a practical way to make counting easier to follow, not a claim backed by formal research.

Try it yourself

Think about it

You have $5 in your Spend jar and receive another $2. How much do you have?

$7. $5 + $2 = $7.

You have three $1 coins and one $2 coin. How much is that?

$5. Three $1 coins make $3, plus the $2 coin makes $5.

You want something that costs $6. You have $4. How much more do you need?

$2 more. $4 + $2 = $6.

For older children

Older kids can take on bigger totals, more mixed denominations, working out simple change, and comparing what they have against the price of something they're saving for — which connects directly to Goals & Saving.

Why it matters

It's more than just maths

Counting money isn't really about arithmetic — it's about answering a question that matters: "How much do I actually have?" Knowing that is what makes real spending and saving decisions possible.

For parents

Three simple activities to try with real coins:

Activity 1

Give them a handful of coins and ask them to count the total.

Activity 2

Ask them to make a particular amount — "Can you make $5? $7? $10?"

Activity 3

Ask them to make the same amount in different ways — "Can you make $5 in three different ways?"

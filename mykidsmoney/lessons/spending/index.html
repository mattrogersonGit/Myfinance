⚖️ MAKING CHOICES · LESSON 1

Spending

When you spend money, you have less left for something else.

The basics

What is spending?

Spending means using your money to buy something — a toy, some food, an activity, anything at all. People spend money because it's how you get things you need or want.

$5You have $5 in your Spend jar.
−$2You spend $2.
$3You now have $3.
Not automatic

Spending is a choice

Having money doesn't mean you have to spend it. Before you buy something, it's worth asking yourself a few questions:

  • Do I really want it?
  • Do I need it?
  • Is it worth the price?
  • Would I rather save the money?
  • What else could I buy with the money?

Spending isn't a bad thing — it's one of the things money is actually for. The goal is just to make it a decision, not something that just happens.

Weighing it up

Choosing one thing means giving up another

You have $10. You could spend it on a toy today. Or you could save it toward something that costs $30. Neither choice is automatically right or wrong — what matters is making the choice on purpose.

A quick preview

Needs and wants

Some spending is on things we need — like food or school supplies. Some is on things we want — like a toy, a game, or an outing. There's a full lesson on this coming later; for now, it's just worth knowing the difference exists.

For parents

A real shopping trip is a great way to practise this. Give your child a small amount of money to manage, and let them:

  1. Choose what they'd like
  2. Check the price
  3. Count their money
  4. Pay for it themselves
  5. See what they have left

This works with an amount you're already comfortable giving them — no need to encourage a purchase that wouldn't otherwise happen. The point is simply for them to feel that spending reduces what they have available.
